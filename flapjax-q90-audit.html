Findings

No recipient has a first BNB funder.

All six recipients are EOAs (zero contract code) with nonce = 0 and a BNB balance of exactly 0 at the block in which they received FLAPJAX. A nonce of 0 means the wallet has never sent a transaction; a zero BNB balance at that block means it had never received BNB either. These wallets had no on-chain history of any kind before the treasury pushed tokens to them.

I also replayed eth_getBalance for each recipient at blocks 131400000, 131420000, 131440000 and 131442015 — all returned 0. One recipient (0xfb2006…) received 0.0005 BNB later, between blocks 131730000 and 131740058, i.e. after the COT day and after the FLAPJAX transfer; that funding is outside this audit's window and its sender is not identifiable from the public record without an archive trace node.

5. Funder groups

Shared funders: 0. Because no recipient has a traceable first BNB funder, no two recipients can be linked through one, and none can be linked to the treasury through BNB funding either.

Distinct operators implied: 6 — one per recipient address. This is the upper bound the chain alone supports. It is worth stating plainly that the chain cannot prove the lower bound: six pre-generated, never-funded wallets receiving tokens in one 2-minute burst from the same treasury is equally consistent with a single operator holding six addresses. The funder test FlapJax uses cannot separate those two cases here, because the test depends on BNB funding that does not exist for any of these wallets.

6. Transfers I could not explain

All 7 transfers are unexplained at the funder level. Each one is fully verifiable as an on-chain event — hash, block, sender, recipient, amount — but none has a BNB funding trail behind its recipient, so none can be attributed to a distinct operator or grouped with any other. Specifically:

• Transfers #1–#5 and #7 (25,000 / 25,000 / 25,000 / 25,000 / 25,000 / 120,000 FLAPJAX) — recipients are unfunded wallets with no prior history.
• Transfer #6 (300,000 FLAPJAX to 0x3aeae5f7…, the same address as #5) — the only repeat recipient. The 300,000 amount is 12× the 25,000 paid to the other four single-transfer recipients, which is consistent with a bonus or a different payout tier, but the chain does not say why.

The concentration is notable: 300,000 of the 545,000 total (55%) went to a single address, and that same address also received one of the four equal 25,000 payments 26 seconds earlier.
